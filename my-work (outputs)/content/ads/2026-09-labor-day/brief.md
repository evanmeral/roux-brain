## 💰 The economics — read this before setting budget

### Allowable CAC (= gross profit per unit; the ceiling on what a sale can cost to acquire)

| Item | Price | At sale price | COGS | **Gross profit = allowable CAC** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 QT Fryer, powered | $285.00 | **$256.50** (−10%) | $153.90 | **$102.60** |
| Cooker Leg Extensions | $119.00 | **$83.30** (−30%) | $71.40 | **$11.90** ⚠️ |
| **Combo order** | $404.00 | **$339.80** | $225.30 | **$114.50** |

**Combo order: 33.7% margin · allowable CAC $114.50 · break-even ROAS 2.97.**

### ⚠️ Leg extensions at 30% off make **$11.90 a unit**
Base margin is 40%, the thinnest in the accessory line. A 30% cut takes it to **14.3%**.

**So: never advertise leg extensions on their own.** Allowable CAC would be $12 — you cannot buy a
customer for that. **Legs are the hook that sells the fryer, not a product to acquire against.**
Every ad should lead with the fryer.

### The good news
Meta's `18qt-TOF-Prospecting` campaign is currently acquiring at **$61.63 per purchase** against a
**$102.60 allowable CAC on the discounted fryer alone** — and $114.50 on the combo. Comfortable
headroom. This is the right product to put money behind.

### Guardrails for the promo window
| Metric | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Break-even ROAS on the combo | **2.97** |
| Stop-loss on blended MER | **below 2.8** |
| Max CAC, fryer-led ads | **$102** |
| Max CAC, combo orders | **$114** |
| Scale rate | ≤20–30% per campaign per week |

### Frame 7 legibility — measured, not eyeballed

The welding shot is brighter and busier than the flame shot it replaced, and it took three passes
to land. First pass was over-corrected: at `brightness(.34)` with two heavy scrims the scene went
black, which defeated the point of swapping it. Second pass at `.50` held the type comfortably but
the welding still didn't read as welding — you caught sparks on the left and not much else.

**Final: `brightness(.86)`, `saturate(.58)`.** The fix was to stop treating the scrim as a global
dimmer. The plate is brightened so the torch, the arc and the gloved hand read at a glance, and the
type bands are protected *locally* instead — the radial stays open through the middle where the arc
is, and the linear does its work from 56% down, heaviest under the small print and footer. That is
why the footer background barely moved (9.3 vs 8.2) while the plate as a whole got much brighter:
**35.5% of the frame is now above luminance 45.**

Saturation came down to `.58` on a second look at phone size: the welder's purple shirt was a large
off-palette field sitting right behind the shield logo and competing with the orange. Muted, it
recedes and the orange `10% off` carries the frame.

⚠️ **`check-centering.py` reports "NUDGE +60" on frame 3. It is wrong, and the nudge was not
applied.** That frame is a photo panel, not a cutout, and the tool's `sat<42` filter is tuned for
bright neutral metal on a dark ground. Test that separates the two explanations:

- The paddle's own row band measures bbox **−0.5**, mass **+42** — dead centre, drifting slightly
  *right*, the opposite direction to the tool's reading.
- The product-free upper band of the same photo (log pile left, cast-iron pot right) measures
  bbox −23.5, mass **−56.4**.

So the −120 is the photo's own luminance spread, not a misplaced subject. The panel is centred by
construction (flex-centred, and the paddle sits at 50.05% of the source image's width). Applying
+60px would have shoved a panel with visible edges off-centre against the copy block below it.

### One thing found while re-rendering, and fixed

Enlarging frames 4 and 6 made a pre-existing artifact obvious: a faint rectangle around the product.
Cause is **not** the cutouts — all four sampled cutouts are genuinely transparent (every off-product
sample reads rgba 0,0,0,0). It is `.pbox{overflow:hidden}` in the shared `brand.css` clipping the
image's `drop-shadow` into a rectangle, which shows as a soft straight edge against the glow.

Fixed in the V3 templates with a scoped `.pbox{overflow:visible}` — the box still sets the layout
footprint, the product does not move (centering numbers above are post-fix), and the shadow is no
longer cut off. **Scoped deliberately, not fixed in `brand.css`:** that file is shared with V2's
already-approved renders and with every other template, so a system-wide change is Nova's call
after the sale.

✅ **Frame 2 now carries the fix too** — Evan unlocked the frame for this one change on 2026-09-08.
All five product frames are consistent; no frame in the swipe has a clipped shadow. Frame 2's copy,
image, size and nudge are otherwise untouched, and its centering measured identically before and
after (+19.0 / −21.0), which is the check that nothing else shifted.

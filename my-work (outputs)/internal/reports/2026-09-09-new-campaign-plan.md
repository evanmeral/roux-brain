# New Advertising Campaign — What We're Doing and Why
**High Performance Cookers · September 9, 2026**
Prepared for Jay and Robert

---

## In one paragraph

We are launching a new Facebook and Instagram campaign on **Friday, September 11th**, at
**$100 a day**. It uses a list of roughly 3,000 people that our advertising partner
IntentWave identified as likely buyers. Facebook takes that list and finds **2.3 to 2.8
million strangers across the country who resemble them**, and we advertise to those people.
This is about finding new customers, not selling again to people who already know us.

---

## Where the audience comes from

IntentWave has software on our website that identifies visitors. Back in August they handed
us a list of about **4,000 contacts** they had identified. Facebook matched **2,700 to
3,200** of those to real accounts.

That list on its own is too small to advertise to profitably. Its value is as a **sample**.
We give Facebook the sample and ask it to go find people who look like them — same buying
habits, same interests, same profile. Facebook does that matching against its own data, which
is far better than any guess we could make about who our customer is.

**This list has been sitting unused in our account since August.** We are putting it to work
now. That is a fair question to put to IntentWave, and we intend to.

---

## What the campaign looks like

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starts** | Friday, September 11 |
| **Budget** | $100 per day |
| **Who it reaches** | 2.3–2.8 million people across the United States, aged 24 and up |
| **Who it deliberately avoids** | Anyone who visited our site in the last 30 days · anyone who bought in the last 90 days · our dealer partners |
| **What it shows** | Three ads: the 120 QT cooker, the Platinum Boiling Bundle, and the 18 QT fryer |

### Why we exclude people

This matters more than it sounds. We already plan a second campaign aimed at people who
visited the site recently. If both campaigns could reach the same person, **we would end up
bidding against ourselves in Facebook's auction** — paying more for the same customer and
making it impossible to tell which campaign actually worked. Excluding recent visitors and
recent buyers from this campaign keeps the two separate and the results readable.

### The three ads

All three are new. None mentions a discount or a code.

1. **120 QT cooker** — how many people one pot feeds, and why that means one pot instead of two
2. **Platinum Boiling Bundle** — the seven-minute boil, and the reviewers who arrived not
   believing it
3. **18 QT fryer** — how fast it gets to frying temperature, pointed at the landing page Evan built

## The budget, and the ceiling on it

**$100 a day for this campaign.** Total Facebook and Instagram spend goes from about $202 a
day to roughly $332 once the second campaign starts.

**We are holding a hard ceiling of $350 a day until we have landed cost and overhead figures.**
That ceiling is deliberately conservative. We know what we make per order in gross terms, but
without landed cost and monthly overhead we cannot calculate the real limit on what a customer
is worth to us. Until then we would rather under-spend than guess.

**This is the constraint we would most like lifted.** On current evidence there is room to
spend more, and we are choosing not to because we cannot prove it yet.

---

## The second campaign — people who already looked

Separately, we are building a campaign aimed at the **7,200 to 8,500 people who visited our
website in the last 30 days** and did not buy. Many of them looked during the Labor Day sale.

This is the warmest group we will have until January, and it shrinks every day as people fall
out of that 30-day window. Budget will be around **$30 a day**.

**We are being upfront that this one is a test, not a proven channel.** At that budget against
that audience size, it will not generate enough data to prove itself in two weeks. We are
spending roughly $420 over a fortnight to answer a question we currently cannot answer at all —
whether advertising to people who already know us pays. That is the whole downside.

---

## How we will judge it

We will not have a verdict in two weeks, and we would rather say that now than produce a
confident-sounding number later.

**What we will watch:**
- **Cost per purchase.** If the new campaign brings customers in near our current $40 average,
  it is working.
- **How often the same person sees our ads.** Above roughly three or four times a week, we are
  over-serving a group that is too small and we cut the budget.
- **Whether total sales move**, not just what Facebook claims credit for. Shopify is the
  scoreboard.

**What would make us stop:** cost per purchase above $60 after four days on the smaller
campaign. Above that it is not paying and we will say so.

---

## What we would ask of you

**Landed cost per unit and monthly overhead.** It has been outstanding since August 28th, and
it is the single thing standing between a cautious advertising budget and a properly
calculated one.

Everything above is being run inside a ceiling we set ourselves because we do not have those
numbers. With them, we can tell you exactly how hard this can be pushed.

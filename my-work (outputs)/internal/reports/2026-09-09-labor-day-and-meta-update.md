# Labor Day Sale Results & Advertising Update
**High Performance Cookers · September 9, 2026**
Prepared for Jay and Robert

---

## The short version

The Labor Day sale **doubled our order count** compared to the same week last year. We took
**126 orders and $41,100.84** in the eight days of the sale, against **63 orders and
$26,144.22** in the same eight days of 2025.

One thing to watch: **the average customer spent less.** Our average order was **$326.20**,
down from **$414.99** last year — a drop of 21.4% on the same kind of discount. We sold to
far more people, but each sale was smaller.

**The last day was the biggest single day.** September 8th alone brought **21 orders and
$7,198.24** — 17.5% of the entire eight-day total in one day, because the discount was about
to expire. That is the clearest lesson in this report and it is worth building on.

---

## The numbers

All figures below come from Shopify and were checked twice on September 9th. "Net sales"
means after discounts and refunds — the money we actually kept.

### The sale versus the same week last year

| | Sept 1–8, 2026 | Sept 1–8, 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Orders** | **126** | 63 | **+100.0%** |
| **Net sales** | **$41,100.84** | $26,144.22 | **+57.2%** |
| **Average order** | **$326.20** | $414.99 | **−21.4%** |

Both years ran a Labor Day sale, so this is a fair like-for-like comparison.

### The sale versus the eight days right before it

| | Sept 1–8 (sale) | Aug 24–31 (no sale) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Orders** | **126** | 65 | **+93.8%** |
| **Net sales** | **$41,100.84** | $25,130.04 | **+63.5%** |
| **Average order** | **$326.20** | $386.62 | **−15.6%** |

This second comparison is arguably the more useful one — same store, same team, same season,
just eight days apart. It tells the same story: **roughly twice the orders, and a smaller
average sale.**

---

## What we take from this

**1. The sale worked.** Doubling orders is not a small result, and it held up against two
different comparisons.

**2. Deadlines work better than discounts.** The final day outperformed every other day of
the sale. People who had been thinking about it bought when the clock ran out. That costs us
nothing — a deadline is free, a discount is not.

**3. The discount is buying volume at the cost of order size.** Average order value fell in
both comparisons. We are attracting more buyers but they are buying smaller. Two Labor Day
sales in a row have now shown this, so it is a pattern rather than a one-off.

**Our recommendation for November: keep the deadline, drop the sitewide percentage off.**
A deadline-driven promotion built around a specific product or bundle, rather than a
blanket discount on everything, should protect order size while keeping the urgency that
clearly works.

## Being straight about what these numbers do and do not show

We would rather under-claim than give you a figure that does not hold up.

- **Facebook's purchase counts are Facebook's own.** It counts a sale as its own if someone
  saw an ad and later bought, even if they would have bought anyway. Shopify is our source of
  truth for revenue; Facebook's figures are useful for comparing ads against each other, not
  for claiming credit for total sales.
- **We cannot yet state a true cost per customer.** That calculation needs our Google
  advertising spend as well, and we do not have a direct feed of that. Any cost-per-customer
  figure we quote today would be understated.
- **We are not reporting which sale creative performed best.** Our own breakdown of the sale
  week turned out to be incomplete, and we would rather re-pull it correctly than repeat a
  conclusion we cannot stand behind. That correction is in progress.
- **The customer new-versus-returning split** we have on file covers September 1–7 only, not
  the full sale, so we have left it out rather than present a partial figure as a whole one.

## The one thing we need

**Landed cost per unit and monthly overhead.** We have been asking since August 28th.

Without it we cannot calculate what we can genuinely afford to spend to acquire a customer.
Every advertising budget decision we make right now is capped conservatively because of that
gap. With those two figures we could tell you precisely how far the advertising can be pushed
before it stops paying — and on current evidence, there is room.

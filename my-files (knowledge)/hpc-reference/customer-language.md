## 🔴 THE #1 OBJECTION — price

**Where it shows up:** sales calls **and public comments on social posts.**

**Verbatim:**
> *"That's way too expensive. Why would I ever buy that?"*

**Why it happens:** a shopper compares a $715 HPC rig to a $200 big-box burner and sees a 3.5× ask
with no way to verify the difference before paying.

**Evan's answer, verbatim:**
> *"they're saving money on propane and time since our tunnel tube technology and burners, if you get
> that as well, will work together to boil faster, re-boil faster, and save a lot of gas in the
> process. Once again, the burner isn't the key focus. It's the tunnel tubes mounted on the bottom
> of the pot."*

---

## ⭐ THE YETI FRAME — the single best objection-killer we have

**Evan's words, verbatim. Do not improve on these:**
> *"It's like buying the really nice Yeti ice chest over a crappy Styrofoam ice chest that only lasts
> a little while. Our products, with good cleaning and care and maintenance, can last for generations.
> This Styrofoam ice chest might work, but you'll just end up buying another one later. The Yeti ice
> chest can last a lifetime."*

**Why this works better than any spec:**
1. It **reframes price as frequency of purchase**, not size of purchase. The question stops being
   "why is this $715?" and becomes "how many $200 pots will you buy?"
2. It borrows a mental model the audience already owns. Nobody in Louisiana needs Yeti explained.
3. It's **emotionally true** — "lasts for generations" is exactly right for a product used at family
   gatherings. The pot outlives the person who bought it. That's a heirloom story, not a spec.
4. It comes from the operator, not a copywriter. It survives the "would a Covington boil host say
   this?" test because a Covington boil host did.

**Deploy it:** PDP above the fold on high-ticket SKUs · price-objection ad creative · abandoned-cart
email #2 · comment replies when someone says "too expensive" · the savings calculator page.

**Careful:** never claim a specific lifespan ("lasts 50 years"). Keep it in Evan's register —
*"with good care, these last generations"* — and let the 5-year warranty carry the hard proof.

## 💡 The two-part price answer

The price objection has **two** answers and they should always run together:

| | Argument | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **1. It costs less to own** | Buy once instead of repeatedly | Yeti frame · 4mm aluminum · 5-yr warranty · "lasts generations" |
| **2. It costs less to run** | Up to 75% less propane, every single boil | Tunnel Tube tech · **the savings calculator we already built** |

> 🔧 **We already own the tool for argument #2 and it's underused.** The propane savings calculator
> exists in `~/Desktop/HPC/Landing Page Code/HPC_Savings_Calculator_Page_3.txt` and
> `commercial-savings-calculator.txt`. It is the direct, quantified answer to the #1 objection in the
> business. It should not be a buried page — it belongs in ads, email, PDPs, and comment replies.

## 🟢 WHY CUSTOMERS BUY (Evan's list, in his order)

1. **Tunnel Tube technology — specifically on the pots.** The pot is the technology, not the burner.
   *"It's the tunnel tubes mounted on the bottom of the pot."*
2. **Customer service.**
3. ⭐ **"We're a small team of 12 that do everything. No big corporate entity here."**
4. **Best in class** across the line. The Boil Boss Triple Jet Burner specifically: fast, saves
   *"almost 75%, maybe even more"* on fuel, Bluetooth electronic ignition.

### The "team of 12" is an under-used asset
A specific number beats a vague claim. "A small team of 12 who do everything" is concrete,
verifiable, human, and **the exact opposite of Bayou Classic's big-box profile.** It pairs with
Made-in-Louisiana to make the origin story a *reason to buy* rather than decoration.

Ways to use it: put the 12 on camera · "built by 12 people in Covington, Louisiana" as an ad line ·
name the person who answers the phone · a shop-floor video from Garrett.

## 🚨 The most important finding in this whole file

**Post-purchase survey, "What made you choose us over other brands?" (n=20):**

| Factor | % |
|---|---|
| **Product Quality** | **80%** |
| Reviews | 25% |
| Fast Shipping | 25% |
| Features | 20% |
| Customer Service | 20% |
| Friend's Recommendation | 15% |
| **Price** | **10%** |

### Price is the #1 objection and almost never the reason anyone buys.
80% choose HPC for **quality**. Only 10% mention price at all.

**What this means for copy: stop defending the price. Lead with quality so hard the price stops
being the question.** Discounting attracts the 10% and erodes margin with the 80%. The Yeti frame
works precisely because it argues *quality economics*, not price.

It also means the December 24.1% discount rate is likely buying customers who were going to buy
anyway (see `my-work (outputs)/internal/reports/2026-08-26-shopify-baseline.md`).

**And note what else is in the top five:** Reviews (25%), Customer Service (20%), Friend's
Recommendation (15%). **Social proof and service outrank price 6:1.** Displaying reviews prominently
and naming the humans who answer the phone is higher-leverage than any promo.

## 🗣️ Verbatims — use these words

> **"Everything was great with the cooker. I got it out of the box and put it together within 10
> minutes. I cooked some steak fingers, fries and okra in it and everything turned out perfect."**
> — Kendall, *18 QT Fish Fryer / Brazier Powered Pot*

⭐ **Steak fingers, fries, and okra. Not crawfish.** A customer describing the year-round use case
unprompted, on HPC's #2 product. This is the de-seasonalization argument in a customer's own words.

> **"Well designed, well packaged. I bought in the off season so I haven't done a boil yet. However,
> the attention to detail on the burner itself is impressive. The 120 qt basket fits snug against
> the pot. (No crawfish leakage). Love the lid hook to hang top on pot. HP Cookers rep met me after
> hours in mandeville at their shop for my pick up. THATS CUSTOMER SEEVICE. Buy with confidence."**
> — Russell, *120 Quart Powered Cooker*

Note: **"no crawfish leakage"** and **"love the lid hook"** — specific, physical details no
copywriter would invent. Also *"bought in the off season"* — off-season buyers exist and are happy.

> **"Does everything advertised to do and more. Heavy duty and built to last."** — Anonymous

> **"Great product, It worked liked advertised."** — John

> **"Awesome products and Service"** — Monty

### 🔑 The pattern in the verbatims: **"it worked like advertised."**
Two separate reviewers volunteer that the product lived up to its claims. People arrive **skeptical
of the 7-minute boil** and are relieved when it's true. That's a copy angle in itself: *the claims
are real, and here's the guy who checked.* Lean into demonstration — timers on camera, unedited.

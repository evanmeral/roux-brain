## ⚠️ BM Digital (now Advantage HQ) — influencer agency. **FAILED. Do not repeat this model.**

**What they were hired to do:** source influencers, monitor whether they actually produced content,
and turn that content into Meta ads.

**What happened — Evan's words:**
> *"in the end they screwed us out of a ton of money and did not have any sales to show for it. They
> were claiming that they generated more than 3/4 of our annual revenue when really they were just
> giving away our product for free to a ton of people who claimed they were going to make content but
> never did."*

**What Evan salvaged:** the handful of influencers who did deliver were moved into HPC's affiliate
program so their existing free gear could keep producing content.

### The three lessons — these are rules now

**1. Vendor-reported attribution must be independently verified.**
Claiming 75% of annual revenue is a claim that could have been checked against total revenue in an
afternoon. **Any vendor number gets reconciled against Shopify before it's believed.** This applies
today to **Coalition** (Google Ads) and to anyone advising on Meta. Not distrust — hygiene.

**2. Never ship product without a signed deliverable.**
The failure mode was free product going out with no contract, no deadline, no consequence. Any future
seeding requires a written deliverable and a date **before** anything ships.

**3. Judge a partner on incremental revenue, not activity.**
Creators contacted, products shipped, and posts promised are activity metrics. They are not results.

> 🔗 **This explains the affiliate master list.** The 75 seeded creators, ~$20K of product, and 44
> unresponsive names are largely **the agency's wreckage, not a program Evan ran badly.** The 24 who
> converted to affiliates are the salvage — and moving them over was the right call.
> See `my-files (knowledge)/hpc-reference/affiliates-influencers.md`.

## 18 QT Fryer Giveaway — Sept 2025. **Mixed. Unmeasured.**

SweepWidget landing page, Instagram carousel, teaser posts. Assets archived in
`~/Desktop/HPC/18qt giveaway/` — reusable as a template.

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Email list growth | ✅ *"a ton of new emails for the email list on klaviyo"* |
| Sales | ❓ Unknown — no way to tell if a buyer saw the giveaway |
| Brand interest | 🤷 *"generated some interest i'm assuming"* |

### 🔑 The verdict isn't unknowable — it's unmeasured, and it's still recoverable
Those subscribers are **still sitting in Klaviyo**. Segment by signup source or signup date window and
check lifetime revenue against a normal-acquisition cohort. That answers "was the giveaway worth it?"
retroactively — and settles whether to run another one. **Worth doing before the next giveaway is
planned.** Owner: Biljana + ROUX, once Klaviyo access exists.

**Guardrails if it runs again:** unique discount code or landing page so purchases are traceable ·
official rules + no-purchase-necessary language + platform disclosure (legal review before launch) ·
require the prize entry to reveal purchase intent (e.g. "which size pot do you need?") so the list
segments itself.

## ⭐ Best thing that's ever worked: **unknown**

Asked directly what has worked best — a post, ad, or promo Evan would point at. Answer:
*"Nothing really comes to mind."*

**That is itself the most important finding in this file.** A brand doing $3M with no identifiable
winner doesn't have a creative problem — it has a **measurement problem.** You can't repeat what you
can't identify. Every campaign starts from zero because none of them left a usable record.

**This is the strongest possible argument for the weekly scoreboard.** Not reporting for its own sake
— it's the mechanism that finally lets HPC say "that one worked, do it again."

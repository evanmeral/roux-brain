## The voice in one line

**A good-hearted south Louisiana person who cooks for a crowd and knows their equipment.**

Bold, playful, warm, confident, a little cheeky. Never a caricature, never a corporation.

## The three dials

| Dial | Setting | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Warmth** | High | Talking to a neighbor across the fence, not to a prospect |
| **Swagger** | Medium-high | We know our pot is faster. We say so. We don't beg. |
| **Polish** | Low-medium | Contractions, sentence fragments, real speech. Not sloppy — just not stiff. |

---

## DO

- **Lead with the culture, let the specs back it up.** Feeling first, number second.
- **Short sentences. Fragments are fine.** Read it out loud — if you'd never say it, cut it.
- **Use real customer language:** *"raging boil in 8 minutes," "game changer," "feeds the whole family."*
- **Second person.** "You." "Your crowd." "Your boil."
- **Concrete nouns:** sacks, ice chests, paper towels, folding tables, the driveway, the back yard.
- **Lifestyle scenes over white backgrounds.** Real boils, real people, real steam.
- **Regional specificity earns trust:** Lent, Good Friday, Mardi Gras parade day, LSU/Saints game day, a fais do-do.

## DON'T

- **Corporate stiffness:** "premium," "solutions," "elevate," "unlock," "revolutionize," "seamless."
- **Cartoon Cajun:** no "cher bébé" pileups, no phonetic accent spelling, no gator mascots talking.
- **Flat guarantees on numbers.** Always "up to," "as little as," "in as fast as."
- **Never knock a competitor by name.** "Thin pots warp" beats naming anyone.
- **No health or safety claims we can't back.**
- **No AI cadence:** em-dash pileups, "it's not just X, it's Y," "in today's world," rhetorical-question openers. Run `stop-slop` on prose before it ships.

## Words we use / words we don't

| Use | Don't use |
|---|---|
| pot, cooker, rig, setup | "unit," "appliance," "cookware system" |
| boil, fry, cook | "culinary experience" |
| crowd, crew, family, cook team | "consumers," "users" |
| fast, hot, tough, built | "premium," "high-end," "luxury" |
| Louisiana / Gulf Coast | "the South" (too vague) |
| Made in USA, built in Louisiana | "domestically manufactured" |
| **rolling boil / raging boil** | ⛔ **"hard boil" — never** |

## ⭐ The Yeti frame — our single best objection-killer

Price is the #1 objection and almost never the reason anyone buys (80% choose on quality, 10% on price).

**Evan's words, verbatim. Do not improve on these:**

> *"It's like buying the really nice Yeti ice chest over a crappy Styrofoam ice chest that only lasts a little while. Our products, with good cleaning and care and maintenance, can last for generations. This Styrofoam ice chest might work, but you'll just end up buying another one later. The Yeti ice chest can last a lifetime."*

**Why it works:** it reframes price as *frequency of purchase*, not size of purchase. The question stops being "why is this $715?" and becomes "how many $200 pots will you buy?" It borrows a mental model the audience already owns, and it's emotionally true — the pot outlives the person who bought it.

**Deploy it:** PDP above the fold on high-ticket SKUs · price-objection creative · abandoned-cart email #2 · comment replies · the savings calculator page.

⚠️ **Never claim a specific lifespan** ("lasts 50 years"). Stay in Evan's register — *"with good care, these last generations"* — and let the 5-year warranty carry the hard proof.

## The two-part price answer — always run both together

| | Argument | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Costs less to own** | Buy once instead of repeatedly | Yeti frame · 4mm aluminum · 5-yr warranty · "lasts generations" |
| **2. Costs less to run** | Up to 75% less propane, every boil | Tunnel Tubes · **the savings calculator we already built** |

## Headline formulas that fit us

1. **Time flex** — "Rolling boil in about 7 minutes. Then do it again in 90 seconds."
2. **Crowd math** — "Two sacks. Forty people. One pot."
3. **Fuel math** — "Same boil. Up to 75% less propane."
4. **Identity** — "Built in Louisiana for people who actually boil."
5. **Objection kill** — "Thin pots warp. Ours is 4mm and warrantied 5 years."
6. **Season trigger** — "Season starts in three weeks. Your pot ready?"
7. **Cheeky** — "Your uncle's burner has been retired. Respectfully."
8. **Quality economics** — "Nobody buys this because it's cheap. They buy it because they're done replacing pots."
9. **Skeptic conversion** — "Everybody thinks the 7-minute thing is marketing. Here's the clock."
10. **Off-season** — "Steak fingers, fries, and okra. It's not just a crawfish pot."
11. **Team of 12** — "Twelve people in Covington, Louisiana build every one of these."
12. **Pot-is-the-tech** — "The speed isn't in the burner. It's in the tubes on the bottom of the pot."
13. **Entry point** — "Already got a burner? You only need the pot."

## The three customer lines to know cold

1. **Objection:** *"That's way too expensive. Why would I ever buy that?"*
2. **Answer:** the Yeti frame — buy once instead of repeatedly, and it costs less to run every boil.
3. **Trust:** *"a small team of 12 that do everything. No big corporate entity here."*

**The "team of 12" is an under-used asset.** A specific number beats a vague claim — concrete, verifiable, human, and the exact opposite of a big-box competitor's profile. Put the 12 on camera. Name the person who answers the phone.

## What the reviews tell us to lean on

- **"It worked like advertised."** Two separate reviewers volunteered this. People arrive **skeptical of the 7-minute boil** and are relieved when it's true. → Lean into demonstration: timers on camera, unedited.
- **"No crawfish leakage." "Love the lid hook."** Specific physical details no copywriter would invent. Use them.
- *"I cooked some steak fingers, fries and okra in it and everything turned out perfect."* — a customer describing the year-round use case unprompted, on our #2 product.

## ⛔ Creative rules — check every creative against all six

1. **Logo always present** unless deliberately omitted for a reason.
2. **Product centred** in whatever area it occupies. Use `prod.py`.
3. **Never "hard boil."** Use **rolling boil** or **raging boil**.
4. **Never link fryers to crawfish.** Match the use case to the product — a fryer is for fish, soft-shell crab, beignets, hushpuppies, fries, wings, okra. Be creative, **never invent** a use case.
5. **Type centred in its container** — stats and labels must not touch borders or dividers.
   **Nothing touches type.** The product never crowds or overlaps a headline unless it's deliberate.
   Leave real clearance between the product stage and the copy block. *(Evan, 2026-09-02.)*
6. **Ask Evan for images you need**, specifying the treatment: cutout, cutout with shadow, plain white background, studio shot, specific angle.

## Claims discipline (non-negotiable)

- Qualify every performance number: "as little as," "up to," "in as fast as."
- **Warranty: full 2-year on all products** (parts and labor, normal use). **Limited 5-year on residential pots 120 QT or smaller** — Tunnel Tube pot bottoms, powered cooker stands and all welds; **owner pays labor and shipping both ways.** Never say "5-year warranty" without both qualifiers: residential, and 120 QT or smaller. Verified against highperformancecookers.com/pages/warranty-information, 2026-09-01.
- Prices must match `what-we-sell.md`. Re-pull before running a price.
- **Tunnel Tube tech is the tubes welded on the pot bottom** — never imply the burner makes it fast.
- Patent No. **11,844,459.** *(The circle logo still says "patent pending" — prefer the shield.)*
- **Lead with quality, not price.**

## Voice checkpoints before anything ships

1. Would a Covington boil host say this sentence out loud? If no, rewrite.
2. Is there a number in it, and is it qualified?
3. Is there a person or a gathering in it, or is it just a product?
4. Does it name a competitor? Cut.
5. Has `stop-slop` been run on it?

## Spelling and register

American spelling throughout (US business). Simple language, short sentences, no jargon unless the audience expects it. Lists for dense information, prose for explanation.
